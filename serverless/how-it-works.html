How a baby monitor app works

Two of your own devices, one minute of setup. No app, no account, nothing stored.

Open babyphone.online on the device that stays with the baby — a spare phone, tablet or laptop — and tap Start baby unit. Allow camera and microphone. You'll see a 6-character room code and a QR code.

What you need before you start

  • Two devices — any combination of phone, tablet or laptop. The one that stays with the baby needs a working camera and microphone.
  • A reasonably current browser on both: Chrome, Safari, Firefox or Edge. Anything that supports WebRTC works, which in practice means most devices from about 2017 onwards.
  • Internet on both devices. It does not have to be the same network — home wifi on one side and 4G or 5G on the other is fine.
  • A charger for the baby device. Streaming video with the screen on will empty a battery in a few hours, so plug it in for the night.

That is the whole list. There is no base station to buy, no app to download from a store, and no account to create.

How a baby monitor over wifi actually works

A classic baby monitor is a pair of radios: the camera unit transmits on a short-range frequency (DECT or 2.4 GHz) and the parent unit receives it. That is why range is measured in metres and thick walls kill the signal.

A baby monitor that runs over wifi works differently. The camera device encodes video and audio and sends it as data over the internet to the parent device. BabyPhone.online uses WebRTC, the same technology that browsers use for video calls, which means three things worth understanding:

  • The stream goes device to device. WebRTC sets up a direct peer-to-peer connection between the two browsers. Your baby's video does not travel through — or get stored on — a server that you would then have to trust.
  • Range is not the limit; internet is. Because the link runs over the internet, the parent device can be in the next room, in the garden, or in a hotel bar two streets away. What matters is that both devices are online, not how far apart they are.
  • It is encrypted end to end by the protocol. WebRTC media is always encrypted (DTLS-SRTP); an unencrypted WebRTC stream is not something a browser will produce.

Two small helpers are needed to get the connection started. A signalling step lets the two devices find each other — that is what the 6-character room code is for. And a STUN server tells each device what its public address looks like from the outside. If your network makes a direct path impossible, an encrypted TURN relay forwards the packets; it can move the stream along but cannot decrypt it. Once the call is up, none of these see or hear anything.

Turning an old phone into a free baby monitor

The most common way people use this is to give a drawer phone a second job. An old handset with a cracked case is still a perfectly good nursery camera: it has a camera, a microphone, a screen you can dim, and wifi. It does not need a SIM card — wifi is enough — and it does not need to be updated to the newest OS, only to have a browser that still gets updates.

Prop it up so it sees the cot, open babyphone.online on it, tap Start baby unit, and leave it plugged in. On your own phone, open the same site and connect with the code. That is a free baby monitor built out of hardware you already own, with nothing bought and nothing thrown away.

Baby monitor app versus a dedicated baby monitor

Neither is simply better; they fail in different ways. Being honest about that is more useful than a sales pitch.

  • Range. A dedicated monitor works anywhere within its radio range and needs no internet at all. An app has effectively unlimited range but needs both devices online.
  • Cost. A monitor with video costs real money and becomes e-waste when the child grows up. BabyPhone.online is free and reuses devices you already have.
  • Travel. A base station plus camera plus two chargers is a lot of bag space. Two phones you were carrying anyway is none.
  • Reliability. A dedicated unit does one job and does it without a browser, an OS update, or a phone call interrupting it. A phone can ring, run out of battery or be pulled into the background by the operating system — which is why the baby device belongs on a charger, with the screen kept awake and other apps left alone.
  • Privacy. Many cloud baby cameras keep footage on a vendor's servers. Cheap analogue monitors can be picked up by a neighbour's receiver. A peer-to-peer browser connection avoids both, though it means you are responsible for who you hand the room code to.
  • Extras. Dedicated units often have real night vision (infrared LEDs), which a phone camera cannot do. A phone, on the other hand, gives you talk-back, lullabies, a cry alert and a night light without extra hardware.

A fair summary: if you want a second monitor, a travel monitor, or you simply want to try video monitoring before spending anything, a browser-based one is hard to beat. If you need something that works with the internet down, buy the box.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to install an app?
No. Everything runs in the browser on both devices.
Is my baby's video stored anywhere?
No. Video and audio stream directly between your own two devices (peer-to-peer via WebRTC), encrypted in transit. Nothing is recorded or seen by a server. Details in the privacy policy.
Does it work when the devices are on different networks?
Yes — wifi at home, 4G/5G on the go. When a direct path is impossible, an encrypted relay forwards the stream without being able to view it.
What does it cost?
Nothing. Every feature is free, there is no paid tier and no account. The site carries no ads and no trackers either, so nothing is being sold on the side.
What do I need?
Two devices with a modern browser (Chrome, Safari, Edge, Firefox). The baby unit needs a camera and microphone; keep it on a charger.
Is a baby monitor app safe?
Safety comes down to where the video goes and who can join. Here the stream runs straight between your two devices, encrypted in transit, so there is no cloud account to be breached and no footage on someone else's server. The baby unit also does not auto-answer: a device that scanned the QR carries a one-time token, and anyone who only typed the room code has to be approved on the baby device's own screen. Like every monitor, it is an aid and not a substitute for checking on your child yourself.
Does a baby monitor work without wifi?
This one needs internet on both devices, but not wifi specifically and not the same network — the baby device can be on wifi while you are on 4G or 5G. With no internet at all on either side, the two devices cannot find each other, and a classic radio monitor would be the better tool.
Can I use an old phone as the camera?
Yes — that is the point. Any spare phone or tablet with a camera, a microphone and an up-to-date browser works, with or without a SIM card. Keep it plugged in.
Does it have night vision?
Not true infrared night vision — no phone camera can do that without extra hardware. In a fully dark room you will hear everything but see very little. The built-in night light gives the baby device's screen a soft warm glow, which is usually enough to make out the cot.
How many people can watch at once?
A second viewer can join with the same room code, but the baby unit asks for approval on its own screen first, so nobody watches without someone in the room agreeing to it.
Safety: BabyPhone.online is an aid, not a replacement for in-person supervision of your baby. Connections depend on your devices and networks and can fail.
